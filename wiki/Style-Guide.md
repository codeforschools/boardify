# Style Guide

This provides a high-level overview how what and how Policies and Regulations should be written.

## General Approach/Tone

### Policy
Whomever prepares the drafts, in the end a specific Policy is approved by the Board and as such should be written from that perspective.  Moreover, it should be written from the highest level.  It is meant to describe the general outcome that is desired, rather than the specific steps taken to get there.  It is the "what", as opposed to the "how".  Policy should be terse, declarative, and strategic.  Unless otherwise required by statute, it should follow the "SORD" (Superintendent or Designee) convention, and direct all actions through the Superintendent rather than "naming names".   Policies generally go through a public reading process and as such are cumbersome and slow to change; this is partially why they should remain as high-level as possible.

### Regulation
If Policies are the "what", Regulations are the "how".  Regulations flow from a particular policy, and are the specific steps that should be taken to achieve the outcome dictated in that policy.  Regulations should be more substantial, imperative, and tactical.  When possible, they should "name names" so that people know exactly to whom they should be directing their questions or actions.  These are written and maintained by the Administration and can be changed by the Administration at will.  Any such changes must be reported to the Board in Open Session, but they are not voted on.  Thus, Regulations are quick to change and can go into more detail accordingly.

## Conventions
Follow these conventions when writing the policy/regulation content.


### Capitalization
Please use Title Case for all titles and section headers.  Avoid use of ALL CAPS.

### Section Headings
Create sections and sub-sections by writing a single line of text in Title Case, preceded by two hash-marks `## Section Title`.  Subsections under that can use three hash marks `### Sub-Section Title`, and so on.  You can continue this pattern as far as necessary.

Effective sectioning is good for organization in general, but in Markdown it has the extra advantage of allowing for auto-creation of tables of contents so that users can easily find what they’re looking for in the file.

### Paragraphs
Paragraphs of text should be soft-wrapped; meaning, they should not have a carriage return in the middle of a paragraph.  Most editors will do this for you automatically.

### Don't Over-Format
Don't overuse Bold, Italics, or Numbering.  Draw extra attention where necessary, but most of the contextual emphasis should be provided by the use of headings.  If a sentence is more than a line or two, it probably shouldn't be bulleted.  And if something is already a heading, it doesn't need to be both a heading and bold.  In short, don't over-compliate things...
